Clinical architecture

How CER-AI Works

CER-AI separates ectasia-risk assessment from surgical tissue safety and keeps major clinical pathways independently interpretable.

01

Independent Risk Pathways

Randleman / ERSS, Final BAD-D, CER-AI-adapted NICE, and PS3 remain distinct rather than being collapsed into a single unsupported score.

02

Pentacam-Based Structure

Important measurements are linked to defined Pentacam source fields and handled consistently across the assessment.

03

Safety Calculations

Tissue safety, pachymetry, treatment magnitude, and procedure-specific constraints are evaluated separately from ectasia scoring.

04

Transparent Reporting

Missing or conflicting information is identified explicitly and the reasoning behind the assessment is documented.

Project history

From HC Ectasia App to CER-AI

The project began as HC Ectasia App, a focused ectasia-screening tool. As it expanded to include multiple risk systems, Pentacam integration, tissue calculations, surgical eligibility, reporting, and planning support, it evolved into CERAI and subsequently the current CER-AI platform.

Clinician-led development

CER-AI was conceived and developed by Hüseyin Cengiz, M.D. from the effort to translate decades of refractive-surgery decision-making into a structured digital framework.

The platform continues to evolve through the interaction of clinical practice, published evidence, and structured software validation.

Clinical philosophy

Artificial intelligence should support clinical judgment, not replace it.

CER-AI organizes and cross-checks clinical information, applies predefined safety rules, and documents the reasoning behind an assessment. The final surgical decision always remains with the surgeon.
