Ectasia-risk evaluation

Multiple independent assessment pathways

CER-AI does not blend the major ectasia-risk systems into one proprietary score. Each pathway is evaluated independently, with its own inputs, interpretation, and procedural consequence.

Randleman / ERSS

Established clinical risk profile

Evaluates the Randleman ectasia-risk framework using the current CER-AI implementation of age, pachymetry, refractive magnitude, residual stromal bed, and anterior topographic evidence.

  • Component-by-component scoring remains visible.
  • Topography evidence is source-tracked rather than inferred silently.
  • The ERSS result remains independent from tomography systems.
Final BAD-D

Independent tomographic signal

The Final BAD-D value is interpreted as its own Pentacam-derived risk channel rather than being converted into Randleman points.

  • Final BAD-D and its classification are displayed directly.
  • BAD subcomponents provide supporting context.
  • An abnormal Final BAD-D can independently alter the final disposition.
CER-AI-adapted NICE

Separate structured tomography pathway

NICE is evaluated independently using the required tomographic inputs and a transparent component audit.

  • Each component and source is retained for review.
  • Missing required information is identified explicitly.
  • The NICE result is not mathematically merged with ERSS or BAD-D.
PS3

Additional procedure-aware risk channel

PS3 adds an independent review of selected corneal, refractive, tomographic, and inter-eye findings.

  • Moderate and High criteria are shown individually.
  • Triggered factors are documented in the report.
  • Procedure-specific consequences for LASIK, PRK, and SMILE remain explicit.
Separate from ectasia scoring: CER-AI also evaluates tissue and procedural safety, including pachymetry, residual stromal calculations, treatment magnitude, postoperative keratometry, prior surgery, and unresolved source conflicts. A reassuring ectasia score does not override a separate safety stop.
Clinical architecture

How CER-AI Works

The platform separates risk assessment, data integrity, and procedure safety so that the final recommendation remains auditable.

01

Independent Risk Pathways

ERSS, Final BAD-D, NICE, and PS3 remain independently interpretable.

02

Pentacam-Based Structure

Important measurements are linked to defined Pentacam source fields and handled consistently.

03

Safety Calculations

Tissue safety and procedure-specific constraints are evaluated separately from ectasia scoring.

04

Transparent Reporting

Missing, conflicting, and surgeon-confirmed information remains visible in the assessment record.

User Guide

Using CER-AI

A practical workflow for qualified ophthalmic professionals using the clinical application.

1

Enter the clinical application

Select Access CER-AI and enter the authorized doctor name in the current supervised trial login flow.

2

Complete case information

Enter patient identification, age, planned procedure, refraction, treatment parameters, clinical modifiers, and other required case inputs.

3

Upload Pentacam material

Upload the required Pentacam screenshots or source images. CER-AI reads designated fields while retaining source provenance.

4

Resolve unread or conflicting fields

If a decision-critical value is unreadable, missing, or conflicting, CER-AI requests completion or surgeon confirmation rather than silently estimating it.

5

Review independent risk systems

Review Randleman / ERSS, Final BAD-D, NICE, and PS3 separately, together with tissue-safety and procedure-specific checks.

6

Review and save the report

Confirm the final disposition, procedure implications, warnings, and planning information before exporting or archiving the case report.

Clinical use: CER-AI is a decision-support system. It does not replace complete examination, image-quality review, clinical judgment, or surgeon responsibility. When the application identifies unresolved decision-critical information, that information should be resolved before relying on the final assessment.

Project history

From HC Ectasia App to CER-AI

The project began as HC Ectasia App, a focused ectasia-screening tool. As it expanded to include multiple risk systems, Pentacam integration, tissue calculations, surgical eligibility, reporting, and planning support, it evolved into CERAI and subsequently the current CER-AI platform.

Clinical philosophy

Artificial intelligence should support clinical judgment, not replace it.

CER-AI organizes and cross-checks clinical information, applies predefined safety rules, and documents the reasoning behind an assessment. The final surgical decision always remains with the surgeon.
