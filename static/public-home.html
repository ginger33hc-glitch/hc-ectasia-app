Ectasia-risk evaluation

Multiple independent assessment pathways

CER-AI does not blend the major ectasia-risk systems into one proprietary score. Each pathway is evaluated independently, with its own inputs, interpretation, and procedural consequence.

Randleman / ERSS

Established clinical risk profile

Evaluates age, pachymetry, refractive magnitude, residual stromal bed, and anterior topographic evidence.

  • Component scoring remains visible.
  • Topography evidence remains source-tracked.
  • ERSS stays independent from tomography systems.
Final BAD-D

Independent tomographic signal

Final BAD-D is interpreted as its own Pentacam-derived risk channel.

  • Final value and class are displayed directly.
  • Subcomponents remain supporting context.
  • Abnormality can independently change disposition.
CER-AI-adapted NICE

Structured tomography pathway

NICE is evaluated independently using required tomographic inputs and a component audit.

  • Each component and source is retained.
  • Missing information is identified.
  • No mathematical blending with ERSS or BAD-D.
PS3

Procedure-aware risk channel

PS3 adds independent review of selected corneal, refractive, tomographic, and inter-eye findings.

  • Moderate and High criteria are shown individually.
  • Triggered factors are documented.
  • LASIK, PRK, and SMILE consequences remain explicit.
Separate from ectasia scoring: CER-AI also evaluates tissue and procedural safety. A reassuring ectasia score does not override a separate safety stop.
Clinical architecture

How CER-AI Works

The platform separates risk assessment, data integrity, and procedure safety so that the final recommendation remains auditable.

01

Independent Risk Pathways

ERSS, Final BAD-D, NICE, and PS3 remain independently interpretable.

02

Pentacam-Based Structure

Important measurements are linked to defined Pentacam source fields.

03

Safety Calculations

Tissue and procedure-specific constraints are evaluated separately.

04

Transparent Reporting

Missing, conflicting, and surgeon-confirmed information remains visible.

User Guide

Using CER-AI

A practical workflow for qualified ophthalmic professionals using the clinical application.

1

Enter the clinical application

Select Access CER-AI and enter the authorized doctor name in the current supervised trial login flow.

2

Complete case information

Enter patient identification, age, planned procedure, refraction, treatment parameters, and clinical modifiers.

3

Upload Pentacam material

Upload required Pentacam screenshots or source images. CER-AI retains source provenance.

4

Resolve unread or conflicting fields

Decision-critical missing or conflicting data are completed or surgeon-confirmed rather than silently estimated.

5

Review independent risk systems

Review ERSS, Final BAD-D, NICE, PS3, and procedure safety separately.

6

Review and save the report

Confirm disposition, procedure implications, warnings, and planning information before export or archive.

Clinical use: CER-AI is a decision-support system and does not replace examination, image-quality review, clinical judgment, or surgeon responsibility.

Clinical philosophy

Artificial intelligence should support clinical judgment, not replace it.

CER-AI organizes and cross-checks clinical information, applies predefined safety rules, and documents the reasoning behind an assessment. The final surgical decision always remains with the surgeon.
